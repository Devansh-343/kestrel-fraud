**To:** Ritu Deshpande, Head of D2C Operations
**Re:** Warranty fraud: what to do next week
**Date:** October 2026

**The decision.** Stop seven outlets' small claims being auto-approved, and use the new review flag to choose which
claims the investigation desk checks. Do not judge the flag by accuracy.

**Why not the 97% accuracy target.** Only about 1 claim in 80 is fraud. A flag that never flags anything is
already "accurate" 97–99% of the time, every month, and stops nothing. Our flag also scores 97–100%, which tells
the board nothing about whether it works. The number that matters is the one Farhan asked for: rupees of fraud
stopped for each claim the desk checks. I suggest we report that to the board instead.

**What the data says about new partners.** You were partly right. Claims from partners onboarded in the last year
are fraudulent about 7 times as often as the rest. But it is not "the new partners". Since the 1 May rule that
auto-approves claims under Rs 2,000, almost all of the small-claim fraud comes from **seven outlets**: SP3160 and
SP3118 (Pune), SP3232 (Jaipur), SP3318 (Hubballi), SP3129 (Chennai), SP3319 (Warangal) and SP3286 (Mysuru).
In May and June they sent 51 of the 1,085 uninspected small claims, and **33 of those 51 were fraud**: 33 of the 35
small-claim frauds found in those two months. Many are priced at exactly Rs 1,995, just under the limit. The other
53 new partners look like everyone else. Meenal is right that most of them are fine.

**The rupees.**
- *The seven outlets:* they submitted Rs 1.13 lakh of uninspected small claims between July and September. At the
  rate seen in May and June, roughly Rs 20,000–25,000 a month of that is fraud we are paying out.
- *The review flag:* tested month by month on January–June 2026, it sent about 9 claims a month to the desk and
  saved a net **Rs 21,000 a month** (Rs 1.27 lakh over six months) after the goodwill cost of holding genuine
  customers and the cost of each review. That is **Rs 2,300 saved per claim checked.** Filling all 40 desk slots
  every month would have saved only Rs 25,600 over the same six months, because most extra reviews cost more in
  goodwill than they recover.
- *Caveat:* the flag earns its money on large claims. Since May, fraud has moved to small claims, where a review
  rarely pays for itself, so in May–June the flag saved very little. That is why the outlet step comes first.

**What it misses.** The flag caught 25 of 67 frauds in the test months but 59% of the fraud *rupees*. It misses
fraud from partners with a clean record, and it needs about a month of confirmed cases before it recognises a new
bad outlet. About half the claims it sends to the desk turn out genuine.

**Next week.**
1. Put the seven outlets back on mandatory inspection for all claims, whatever the amount (Meenal's team).
2. Ask the desk to work the flag's queue: about 15 claims a month on current data, not 40. Use the spare
   capacity to audit the seven outlets' July–September claims.
3. Watch for claims priced between Rs 1,900 and Rs 2,000 from any outlet. If the seven are blocked, others may copy.
4. Close the open investigations faster. Every confirmed outcome makes the flag better within a month.
5. Report "rupees saved per claim checked" to the board alongside accuracy, with this memo's explanation.
